Study of Seasonal Trends in Ocean Characteristics and
Acidification in Al Hoceima Bay (Central Rif, Morocco)
Abdelghafour Hrida1 **, Issam Etebaai1 and Morad Taher1
*, Issam Etebaai1 and Morad Taher1
1 Team of Applied Geosciences and Geological Engineering, Faculty of Science and Technology of Al-Hoceima, Abdelmalek Essaadi
University, Tetouan, Morocco
Abstract. The Al Hoceima,bay as a vital marine ecosystem, faces the challenge of ocean acidification, a
major consequence of climate change. This study analyzes the variation of three key oceanic parameters -
Sea Surface Temperature (SST), seawater pH, and Dissolved Inorganic Carbon (DIC) - over a 12-year
period, from 2010 to 2021, utilizing an advanced statistical approach. The results reveal distinct seasonal
trends and specific noteworthy years. Ocean acidification may pose a threat to the marine biodiversity of
the region. This analysis is crucial for comprehending and mitigating the impacts of climate change on the
Bay of Al Hoceima.
Keywords. Climate change, Al Hoceima bay, Acidification, Marine ecosystem, Oceanic parameters

2.2 Data Collection
Ocean acidification is a major environmental concern
due to its impact on marine biodiversity and coastal
ecosystems [1]. This acidification is primarily caused by
increased absorption of atmospheric carbon dioxide
(CO2) by the oceans, resulting in a decrease in seawater
pH[2]. Al Hoceima Bay harbors an exceptional variety
of marine species, from fish to mollusks to
phytoplankton [3]. These species depend closely on the
local marine environment, including parameters such as
sea surface temperature (SST), seawater pH, and
dissolved inorganic carbon concentration (DIC). Ocean
acidification in the bay could disrupt their natural
habitat, influence their growth and reproduction, and
potentially lead to changes in species composition [4].
In this context, this study aims to assess the distribution
of these key oceanic parameters in Al Hoceima Bay over
time using advanced statistical methods. Our main
objective is to better understand the potential
acidification of this crucial coastal area and analyze its
impact on local marine biodiversity. This knowledge is
essential for the management and conservation of this
sensitive coastal ecosystem.
2 Methodology

Copernicus platform [7], an invaluable resource for
accessing global oceanographic data. Over a 12-year
period, from 2010 to 2021, and within a spatial range
spanning from 35.272N to 35.21S in latitude and from -
3.89W to -3.77E in longitude, a variety of data was
gathered. These data encompass three essential oceanic
parameters: Sea Surface Temperature (SST), seawater
pH,
and

observations, providing exceptional spatial resolution at
1 km x 1 km. Measurements of seawater pH, as well as
levels of Dissolved Inorganic Carbon (DIC), were also
collected from satellite observations, benefiting from a
spatial resolution of 4 km x 4 km.

3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Analysis of Annual Trends
Upon closer examination of the annual graphs of the
three parameters, we observed a nonlinear evolution
over time, as depicted in the (Fig 3), indicating
significant seasonal or cyclical variations in these
parameters. Let's begin with Sea Surface Temperature
(SST). We identified three distinct phases in its yearly
evolution. At the beginning of the year, SST generally
ranged between 288 and 291 kelvins. Then, in the
second stage, SST gradually increased until reaching its
peak in the middle of the year before starting to
decrease, suggesting a seasonal variation. Regarding
seawater pH, we observed a general variation between
7.92 and 8.12 throughout the year. This variation also
exhibited three distinct phases, indicating a seasonal
trend. We observed a drop in pH during the summer
season, which is a notable aspect of this seasonal
variation. As for Dissolved Inorganic Carbon (DIC)
concentration, we noted an interesting seasonal trend.
Although DIC appears to follow a general trend
inversely related to pH, it's not a direct relationship. For
instance, when pH decreases, DIC tends to increase, but
the correlation between the two parameters isn't
perfectly linear. This suggests that other factors, such as
ocean circulation and nutrient inputs, may also influence
the DIC concentration in the bay.

4 Conclusion
This analysis sheds light on the seasonal variability and
key years in the oceanic parameters of the Bay of Al
Hoceima,
highlighting
the
potential
impact
of
acidification due to climate change. Protecting this
crucial ecosystem requires ongoing monitoring and
adaptation measures. Conservation efforts must be
strengthened to preserve the marine biodiversity of the
region. In perspective, international collaboration is
essential for a comprehensive. Understanding of these
trends and coordinated action to counter the effects of
acidification.
